\paragraph{Monitoring prior convergence.}
Inception-based FID is not meaningful for log-mel spectrograms, so we track each
generator with a mel-native analogue. For each dataset we train a small CNN
classifier (four stride-2 convolution blocks, global average pooling, and a 128-d
embedding) on the real mel spectrograms of the training speakers only. We train one
classifier for \emph{speaker identity} ($K{=}50$ speakers for AudioMNIST,
$K{=}2{,}418$ for Speech Commands) and one for \emph{content} (10 digits for
AudioMNIST, 35 keywords for Speech Commands). Every 10k iterations we draw
$N{=}2{,}000$ samples from the EMA generator with a fixed seed. The seed freezes both
the latent codes and the per-layer noise, so differences between checkpoints reflect
the generator alone. For each classifier we then compute two statistics:
(i)~the Fr\'echet distance (FD) between Gaussian fits to the 128-d embeddings of
generated and real mel spectrograms, which measures distributional fidelity; and
(ii)~the normalized coverage entropy $H(\hat{p})/\log K$ of the histogram $\hat{p}$ of
predicted classes over the $N$ samples, which drops when the generator collapses onto
a subset of speakers or words. The real-data reference is the same entropy computed on
the training set. For Speech Commands, $K > N$ caps the attainable entropy at
$\log N/\log K = 0.976$, so we resample the real histogram to $N$ samples and give
both sides the same ceiling. The speaker metrics are our primary signal: content
classifiers saturate and cannot detect a collapse of the speaker manifold, and speaker
diversity is what a prior needs to reconstruct speakers it never saw. Each dataset
has its own classifier, so FD values can be compared only along a single curve, not
across datasets or with image FID. For the Speech Commands prior we also measure how
the 35-way keyword classifier labels its samples on the ten digit words
(\emph{zero}--\emph{nine}). We report the fraction of samples assigned to a digit and
the normalized entropy over the ten digits. This is the sub-manifold that matters when
the prior is used out of distribution on AudioMNIST targets.

\paragraph{Convergence curves.}
Figures~\ref{fig:conv-am} and~\ref{fig:conv-sc} show both statistics over training.
On AudioMNIST, speaker FD drops by an order of magnitude within the first 110k
iterations (32.0 to 3.8) and reaches its minimum of 3.05 at 300k. Over the same span,
speaker coverage entropy rises from 0.857 to 0.977, within 0.010 of the real-data
value (0.987). Digit FD falls from 1.88 to 0.46, and digit entropy stays above 0.99
from 30k onward (real: 0.998). Content coverage therefore saturates early, and later
training mainly improves speaker coverage. A single excursion at 230k (speaker FD
27.2, speaker entropy 0.896) recovers by the next checkpoint. On Speech Commands,
speaker FD falls 20-fold, from 247 at 10k to 12.6 at 400k, and stays between 12.1
and 14.6 from 320k onward. Speaker entropy plateaus between 0.79 and 0.81 from 160k
onward, against a size-matched real-data value of 0.848. Keyword FD falls from 12.5
to 0.55, and keyword entropy rises from 0.777 to 0.927 (real: 0.938).
Figure~\ref{fig:conv-sc-digits} shows the digit sub-manifold. The fraction of samples
classified as a digit settles at $0.389 \pm 0.008$ (mean $\pm$ s.d.\ over the last
ten checkpoints), matching the 0.384 that the same classifier assigns to real
training data. Digit entropy reaches 0.967 (real: 0.972). The prior thus gives digits
the same share of its samples as the data does and spreads them almost as evenly
across the ten digits. All real-data references are computed on the classifiers' own
training data, so they are optimistic targets.

\paragraph{Checkpoint selection.}
We select checkpoints on the speaker metrics, then check that the content metrics are
at or near their best. For AudioMNIST we use the 300k checkpoint. It has both the
lowest speaker FD (3.05) and the highest speaker coverage entropy (0.977) of the run,
as well as the highest digit entropy (0.997). Its digit FD (0.457) is within 4\% of
the run minimum (0.441 at 210k). For Speech Commands we use the final 400k
checkpoint. Its speaker FD (12.6) is within 5\% of the run minimum (12.1 at 360k).
That gap is smaller than the checkpoint-to-checkpoint variation on the plateau
(s.d.\ 1.1 over the last ten checkpoints), so we prefer the longest-trained model.
It also has the highest keyword entropy of the run (0.927), and its digit fraction
(0.389) and digit entropy (0.967) match the real-data values (0.384 and 0.972).
